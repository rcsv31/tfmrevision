The current investigation examined the different eye movements used by
defenders during spike serve reception in a volleyball game. Six expert and six
novice players participated in the experiment. The server exhibited randomized
server techniques (spike and ordinary server technique) to defenders. The study
required having seven perfect serves received by a defender for data collection from
mobile eye trackers to be able to measure/analyze the defender's eye movements. We
analysed the defender's eye movements during the folloiinng time period: the time
started when a server tossed a ball in the air in preparation to serve to a defonder
and stopped when the defonder made contact with the ball. Vie time phases were
classified into tivo categories: ball toss phase and fiight phase. The results indicated
that expert players were more focused an the server's shoulder and arm region than
novice players during the ball toss phase, and experts were faster at tracking a ball
than novice players during the fiight phase.

The process of acquiring and utilizing the advanced visual cues can be examined

The vistjal search pattem is defined as the way in which performers continuously
move their eyes to focus on important display features, thereby enabling them to

has also been used as a means of discriminating performance expertise in sport
tasks (Cauraugh & Janelle, 2002). From previous studies, it has been shown that

more efficient visual search patterns for acquiring proper visual cues than less
skilled players in various sports such as tennis (Farrow et al., 1996), badminton

Methods
Participants
Of 12 participants, all reported they had normal vision. Six of these subjects
were tnale defensive players (M=23.17±L45 years of age) of the Hanyang
University volleyball team (expert group), and the other six subjects (M=20.35±.82

Apparatus
An Applied Science Laboratories (ASL) Mobile Eye movement system was used
to collect the eye movement and ball-tracking data (Applied Science Laboratories
Waltham, MA). The video-based monocular system measures eye line of gaze with
respect to a head-mounted camera by computing the relative positions of two
feattires of the eye, the pupil and the corneal reflection (a reflection of a near
infrared light source from the surface of the cornea), in relation to the optics. The
ASL system computes the line of gaze by measuring the vertical and horizontal
distances between the center of the pupil and the comeal reflection after correcting
for second-order effects. To assess the exact location of gaze, the 4000 SU
processor superimposed a white cursor representing 1 degree of visual angle on the
video image produced by the scene camera. Those images were recorded by a video
recorder (Sony Corp., Tokyo, Japan, and Model GV-DIOOOE) and used for data

Dependent Variables and Data Analysis
Two phases of each trial were defined. The ball toss phase began with the first
ftame showing the upward movement of the ball as it was tossed by the server and
continued until the hand contacted the ball. The flight phase began with the ftame
showing the ball leaving the server's hand and continued until the hall contacted the

1987). As in previous studies (Vickers, 1996), a fixation was defined when the gaze
remained stable over at least 100 ms (3 frames). A saccade was coded when a rapid
shift of the gaze was observed from one location to another, with a minimtim
duration of 66.6ms or more. Pursuit tracking occurred when the gaze was
maintained on the hall within 3 degrees of visual angle for a minimum of 3 firames.
