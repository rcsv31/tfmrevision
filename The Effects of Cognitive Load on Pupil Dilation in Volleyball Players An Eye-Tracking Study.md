Abstract: Volleyball is a sport that demands not only high levels of physical exertion but also 
significant cognitive engagement. Rapid decision-making, attentional control, and visuomotor 
coordination are essential for optimal performance. This study investigates the effects of 
cognitive load on pupil dilation and reaction time in competitive volleyball athletes. A withinsubjects, controlled experimental design was employed, involving 26 female volleyball players 
(age 15.4 ± 1.2 years) who regularly train and compete at the regional level. Participants 
completed three cognitive load conditions in a randomized order: (P1) a motor task requiring 
the suppression of randomly illuminated lights as quickly as possible, (P2) a cue-based task in 
which the next light always appeared in the same color zone as the previous one, allowing for 
anticipatory responses, and (P3) a mixed cue task that introduced a more mixed cue structure, 
increasing task difficulty and necessitating greater motor control adaptation. Performance was 
assessed using the Fitlight system, which involved deactivating 25 lights under three distinct 
levels of cognitive load. To ensure the precise evaluation of cognitive load effects, pupil dilation 
was continuously measured using Tobii Pro Glasses 2. Results demonstrated that pupil dilation 
increased significantly under higher cognitive load conditions (p < .001) and that reaction times 
were faster when predictive cues were available (p < .001). These findings suggest that cognitive 
load plays a critical role in shaping athletes’ visual and motor responses, with direct implications 
for sports training and decision-making processes. Enhancing cognitive training protocols in 
volleyball may improve athletes’ ability to process visual information efficiently and execute 
rapid motor responses under high-pressure conditions. 
Keywords: cognitive load; volleyball; vision, pupil, eye tracking

tactical strategies to optimize decisionmaking processes (Furley & Memmert, 2013).

capacity of working memory and cognitive 
resources (Adams et al., 2018; Chai et al., 
2018) may result in excessive cognitive load

impacting learning processes and task 
performance (Paas et al., 2003). Additionally, 
a 
cognitive 
load 
that 
surpasses 
an 
individual's capacity can lead to heightened 
arousal, 
increasing 
stress 
levels 
and 
potentially 
impairing 
performance 
depending on the ability to maintain focus. 
Consequently, while moderate arousal levels 
enhance performance, excessive cognitive 
load can contribute to mental fatigue and 
performance decline over time (Arent & 
Landers, 2003; Yerkes & Dodson, 1908). 
Therefore, effective management of cognitive 
load in athletes is critical for enhancing 
decision-making 
speed 
and 
accuracy, 
ultimately optimizing game performance. 
The literature indicates that athletes engaged 
in 
open-skill 
sports 
possess 
higher 
attentional capacity and utilize cognitive 
processes such as visuospatial attention and 
problem-solving more efficiently (Gu et al.,

elite 
athletes 
exhibit 
superior anticipation and decision-making 
skills compared to novices by detecting 
environmental cues earlier and processing 
information more efficiently (X. Wang et al., 
2023; Williams, 2002; Xu et al., 2021). In sports 
with high cognitive demands, such as 
volleyball, 
cognitive 
load 
management 
should be a fundamental component of 
training 
programs.

One of the most widely used

cognitive 
effort 
during 
mental 
tasks. 
Subsequent studies have reinforced the use 
of pupil diameter as a robust indicator across 
various levels of cognitive load (Van Der Wel 
& Van Steenbergen, 2018). Jerčić et al. (2020) 
observed that players exhibited physiological 
responses such as increased heart rate and 
pupil dilation while performing cognitively 
demanding game tasks (Jerčić et al., 2020). 
These findings indicate that studies aiming to 
evaluate cognitive activity levels can rely 
solely on pupil diameter measurements and 
that pupillary responses effectively reflect 
physiological arousal levels, facilitating the 
detection of such activities (Jerčić et al., 2020). 
Given the effects of cognitive functions on 
motor performance, it is crucial to gain a 
deeper 
understanding 
of 
the 
motor 
performance and physiological responses 
exhibited by athletes under cognitive load 
conditions of varying difficulty. 
There 
is 
a 
well-established 
relationship between cognitive functions and 
motor performance in volleyball players 
(Formenti et al., 2022; Trecroci et al., 2021). 
However, a comprehensive understanding of 
the impact of cognitive cues on attentional 
control and motor performance in volleyball 
players remains limited. One of the key 
strengths of this study is its holistic approach, 
integrating objective measurement methods 
through 
a 
simultaneous 
reaction 
measurement 
system 
to 
provide 
both 
potential benefits and a deeper insight into 
the limitations of existing research. This 
study aims to provide a comprehensive 
evaluation of the effects of cognitive load on 
pupil diameter and reaction performance in 
volleyball athletes across three tasks with 
varying levels of cognitive cues in addition to 
a motor task: (1st Test: motor task (P1), 2nd 
Test: 
motor+cue 
task 
(P2), 
3rd 
Test: 
motor+mixed cue task (P3)). The study 
hypotheses are: (i) Reaction times will be 
shorter in tasks involving cognitive cues (P2 
and P3) compared to the test involving only 
the motor task (P1), (ii) Due to learning 
effects, the performance time in the mixed 
cognitive cue condition (P3) will remain the 
same as P2 despite increased cognitive load, 
(iii) Pupil diameter will increase as task 
difficulty increases, (iv) Greater cognitive 
load allocation in P3 compared to P2 will lead 
to a more pronounced increase in pupil 
diameter.

Apparatus and Task - The study's data 
were collected using the FitLight Trainer™ 
system (FitLight Sports Corp., Canada), 
which consists of eight LED lights designed 
to measure participants' reaction times. The 
LED lights in this system, which are 
controlled by a tablet computer, can be set to 
different colors, and their flashing duration 
can be customized. Participants' pupil 
diameters were recorded using Tobii Pro 
Glasses 2 (Stockholm, Sweden), which 
feature 12 infrared projections reflecting off 
the cornea. The glasses are equipped with 
four micro-cameras—two on the right and 
two on the left—positioned along the inner

camera located at the front center of the 
glasses 
to

environment. 
Additionally, a microphone is situated 
beneath the main camera of the video 
recording glasses. The system operates at a 
frequency of 100 Hz with a deviation of 0.5 
degrees. Raw data from the FitLight Trainer 
system (reaction times) and Tobii Pro Glasses 
2 (pupil dilation) were stored on their 
respective software platforms. Reaction time 
data were automatically recorded and 
transferred to Excel for preprocessing. Pupil 
dilation data were processed using Tobii Pro 
Lab software, which established a baseline 
pupil size and extracted stimulus-related 
changes. 
Subsequently, 
the 
data 
were 
examined for missing values and artifacts, 
and statistical analyses were conducted using 
IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0). These 
methodological steps ensured high data

and

observed in the P1, P2, and P3 tasks with 
varying difficulty levels of cognitive cues 
compared to the baseline condition. It was 
found that this dilation was greater in the P2 
task 
condition, 
where 
cognitive 
load 
increased, compared to P1. However, no 
statistically significant difference was found 
between the P1-P3 and P2-P3 tasks. In our 
study, while reaction time performance 
decreased in tasks with increased cognitive 
load, it is considered that pupil diameter may 
have expanded due to the increased 
cognitive load during motor tasks combined 
with cognitive cues. Overall, our results align 
with previous studies reporting that pupil 
dilation is associated with higher cognitive 
load and greater visual attention (Appel et 
al., 2018; Duchowski et al., 2018; Parikh et al., 
2018). However, the prediction that pupil 
dilation would be greater in the P3 task, 
where the cognitive cue was more complex, 
compared to the P2 task, was not confirmed. 
This finding may be explained by various 
mediating factors that could influence how 
athletes process cognitive cues and execute 
motor responses. One possible mediating 
factor is attentional control. The extent to 
which athletes can focus on relevant cues 
may influence performance outcomes by 
determining how cognitive resources are 
allocated (Furley & Wood, 2016). The mixed 
cues in the P3 task may not have generated 
additional 
cognitive 
load 
if 
athletes 
optimized their attentional resources due to 
the learning effects from the P2 task. 
Additionally, physiological arousal levels 
could also be a significant factor shaping the 
effects observed in this study. The mixed cue 
in the P3 task may have led to high arousal 
by increasing stress levels, which, depending 
on the ability to maintain focus, could impair 
performance (Arent & Landers, 2003; Yerkes 
& Dodson, 1908). This heightened arousal 
might 
have 
caused 
mental 
fatigue, 
preventing further pupil dilation. These 
factors provide an important indicator for 
understanding how working memory may 
be linked to physiological responses in the P3 
task. 
 
In conclusion, this study evaluated 
how volleyball athletes respond to cognitive 
cues presented during motor tasks and 
examined the effects of these cues on 
cognitive 
load 
by 
assessing 
cognitive 
indicators such as reaction time and pupil 
diameter. Our findings demonstrate that 
athletes 
accelerate 
their 
information 
processing and significantly shorten their 
reaction times by utilizing cognitive cues. 
Additionally, it was observed that pupil 
diameter 
expanded 
as 
cognitive 
load 
increased, but this effect could be balanced 
when the complexity of the cue increased. 
This 
finding 
suggests 
that 
mediating 
mechanisms such as cognitive resource 
allocation, 
attentional 
control, 
and 
physiological arousal levels play a critical 
role in sports performance. Therefore, our 
findings and research methodology provide 
valuable insights that could inform future 
research in this field.

effects 
of 
cognitive 
cues 
on 
sports 
performance in more depth by considering

The findings of this study provide 
valuable insights into the interaction between

training

The

scenarios.

the

of 
personalized

programs that reduce cognitive overload.

pupil diameter; however, further research is 
needed to fully validate the effects of 
cognitive load on pupil dilation.

The results indicated that volleyball

reaction times when performing tasks with 
cognitive cues. This study highlights the 
importance of attentional control toward 
relevant cues and cognitive load in shaping 
motor performance in volleyball athletes. The 
findings indicate that integrating cognitive 
demands into training can enhance reaction 
times, improve attentional efficiency, and 
ultimately boost real-game performance 
under pressure. Sports scientists and coaches 
can optimize training protocols to maximize 
cognitive and motor efficiency by using pupil 
dilation as an indicator of cognitive effort. By 
doing so, they can ensure that athletes 
perform 
at 
their 
best 
in 
high-stakes 
environments.
